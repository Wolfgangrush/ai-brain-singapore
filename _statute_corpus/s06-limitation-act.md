## Topic Digest

### A. The Core 6-Year Period (Section 6)
Actions founded on contract or on tort (excluding personal injury) must be commenced within **6 years** from the date the cause of action accrued.

### B. Personal Injury — 3 Years (Section 24A)
Personal-injury actions must be commenced within **3 years** from the later of:
- the date the cause of action accrued, or
- the **date of knowledge** of the person injured.

"Date of knowledge" is a defined concept turning on what the plaintiff knew or ought reasonably to have known about the injury, its attribution, and the identity of the defendant.

### C. Recovery of Land — 12 Years (Section 9)
Actions to recover land must be commenced within **12 years** from the date the right of action accrued.

### D. Judgment Debts — 12 Years (Section 6(3))
Actions to enforce a judgment must be commenced within **12 years** from the date the judgment became enforceable. Arrears of interest on judgment debts are recoverable for the preceding 6 years only.

### E. Trust Property — Section 22
**No limitation period** applies to actions against a trustee:
- for fraud or fraudulent breach of trust to which the trustee was party or privy; OR
- to recover trust property (or proceeds) in the trustee's possession or previously received and converted to the trustee's use.

### F. Disability — Section 24
If, at the date the cause of action accrued, the person entitled was under a **disability** (minority or unsound mind), the period begins to run only when the disability ends or the person dies, whichever comes first.

### G. Fraud and Concealment — Section 29
Where the action is **based on the fraud** of the defendant (or someone for whom the defendant is responsible), or where the defendant has **deliberately concealed** facts relevant to the plaintiff's right of action, time does not begin to run until the plaintiff discovers the fraud / concealment, or could with reasonable diligence have discovered it.

## Verbatim Anchor Quote
> "The following actions shall not be brought after the expiration of 6 years from the date on which the cause of action accrued: (a) actions founded on a contract or on tort …"
> — Section 6(1), Limitation Act 1959.

## Cross-statute Relationships
- **Contract Law** — accrual on breach (see [s02-contract-law-overview.md](./s02-contract-law-overview.md)).
- **Tort Law** — accrual on damage / on the date the tort was complete (see [s11-tort-law-overview.md](./s11-tort-law-overview.md)).
- **Defamation Act 1957** — special **1-year** limitation period for defamation (not in s 6 LA).
- **Civil Liability for Maritime Claims Act 1995** — separate periods for maritime claims.
- **IRDA 2018** — bankruptcy / winding-up proof-of-debt is a separate procedural regime.

## Practical Implications for a Solo Advocate
- **Intake checklist:** every new file gets a "limitation accrual date" entered into the docket on the day of intake. The single most common professional-negligence claim against advocates worldwide is a missed limitation.
- **Date of knowledge** (PI cases) is frequently disputed; collect contemporaneous records (medical files, employer correspondence) before they become hard to retrieve.
- **Acknowledgment / part-payment** (s 26) can re-start time on contract / debt claims — surface this in debt-recovery strategy.
- **Defamation 1-year:** rapid action required; the general 6-year tort period does NOT apply.
