## Topic Digest

### A. Formation
A contract is formed where four elements coincide:
1. **Offer** — a definite expression of willingness to be bound on stated terms.
2. **Acceptance** — unequivocal assent to the offer's terms, communicated to the offeror.
3. **Consideration** — something of value moving from the promisee (or detriment suffered).
4. **Intention to Create Legal Relations** — presumed in commercial contexts; rebuttable in domestic/social contexts.

### B. Statutory Refinements
1. **Contracts (Rights of Third Parties) Act 2001 (CRTPA)** — A non-party can enforce a term of a contract if (a) the contract expressly so provides, or (b) the term purports to confer a benefit on that third party (subject to a contrary-intention exception).
2. **Misrepresentation Act 1967** — Civil liability for false statements that induced contract entry (extends remedies beyond common-law deceit, including damages in lieu of rescission).
3. **Unfair Contract Terms Act 1977 (UCTA)** — Restricts the ability to exclude or limit liability for negligence, breach of contract, and (in consumer or standard-form contexts) other terms; applies a "reasonableness" test.
4. **Electronic Transactions Act 2010 (ETA)** — Confirms validity of contracts formed electronically and electronic signatures; certain document classes (Wills · deeds for immovable property · most negotiable instruments · most powers of attorney) are excluded from electronic execution (see [s17-eta-electronic-transactions.md](./s17-eta-electronic-transactions.md)).

### C. Vitiating Factors
Includes Mistake (common, mutual, unilateral), Misrepresentation (fraudulent, negligent, innocent), Duress (economic and physical), Undue Influence (actual and presumed), and Illegality / public-policy invalidity.

### D. Discharge
Contracts are discharged by:
- **Performance** — both parties fully perform.
- **Agreement** — accord and satisfaction; novation; rescission.
- **Frustration** — supervening event that renders performance radically different (narrow doctrine).
- **Breach** — repudiatory breach gives the innocent party the right to terminate and sue for damages.

### E. Remedies
- **Damages** — the primary common-law remedy; aim is the expectation measure (put the plaintiff in the position they would have been in had the contract been performed). Subject to remoteness (Hadley v Baxendale principles), mitigation, and the rule against penalty clauses.
- **Specific Performance** — equitable; discretionary; not available where damages are an adequate remedy. Commonly used for unique chattels and real estate.
- **Injunction** — equitable; commonly used to enforce negative covenants (e.g., restraint-of-trade clauses, subject to public-policy review).

### F. Interpretation
Singapore courts apply a **contextual approach** to contract interpretation — the contract is read against its admissible factual matrix at the time of formation. Subjective intention is not admissible; objective ascertainment of the parties' presumed intention from the words used and the context governs.

## Verbatim Anchor Quote
> "A person who is not a party to a contract may in his or her own right enforce a term of the contract if — (a) the contract expressly provides that he or she may; or (b) … the term purports to confer a benefit on him or her."
> — Section 2, Contracts (Rights of Third Parties) Act 2001.

## Practical Implications for a Solo Advocate
- **CRTPA exclusion clause:** unless third-party enforcement is intended, every commercial contract should include an express CRTPA exclusion clause.
- **UCTA reasonableness:** standard-form contracts (engagement letters, MSAs) must be drafted so each exclusion / limitation clause survives the UCTA reasonableness test.
- **Limitation discipline:** docket every contract dispute with the 6-year accrual date on intake; missed limitation is the single most common source of professional negligence claims (cross-ref s06).
- **Frustration vs force majeure:** common-law frustration is narrow; commercial parties should rely on contractual force-majeure clauses rather than the doctrine.
