## Topic Digest

### A. Capacity Definition — Section 4
A person lacks capacity if, at the material time, they are **unable to make a decision for themselves** in relation to a matter **because of an impairment of, or a disturbance in the functioning of, the mind or brain**. Inability is decision-specific and time-specific — capacity is NOT an all-or-nothing global status.

### B. Presumption of Capacity — Section 3
> "A person must be assumed to have capacity unless it is established that he or she lacks capacity."
The burden of establishing lack of capacity falls on the person asserting it.

### C. Lasting Power of Attorney (LPA)
- A **Donor** appoints one or more **Donees** to make decisions on the Donor's behalf if the Donor loses capacity.
- Scope of authority: **Property & Affairs** (financial / asset management); and / or **Personal Welfare** (healthcare / accommodation / day-to-day welfare).
- **Form 1** — the standard form prescribed by OPG (most common, restricted scope of authority).
- **Form 2** — bespoke (customised scope; lawyer-drafted).
- **Registration** — LPA must be registered with the OPG to take effect.
- **Certificate Issuer (CI)** — a legally qualified person (or other prescribed professional) certifies the Donor's understanding and the absence of pressure / fraud at the time of execution.

### D. Court-Appointed Deputies
Where a person has lost capacity **without** having executed an LPA, the **Family Justice Courts** may appoint a **Deputy** to make decisions on the person's behalf. Deputies are subject to OPG supervision, scope-of-authority limits, and accounting / reporting obligations.

### E. Best Interests Principle — Section 6
Any act done or decision made for a person lacking capacity **must be in their best interests**. The Act sets out a non-exhaustive checklist — including the person's past and present wishes / values / beliefs; involvement of carers, family, and the person themselves; and avoidance of discrimination based solely on age, appearance, or condition.

### F. Office of the Public Guardian (OPG)
- Registers LPAs.
- Supervises Deputies (including required accounting / reporting).
- Investigates complaints about Donees / Deputies.
- Maintains a Code of Practice for Donees, Deputies, and professionals.

### G. Statutory Wills
The Family Justice Courts can authorise the making of a statutory Will on behalf of a person who lacks the testamentary capacity to make one themselves. This sits alongside the Wills Act 1838 framework (see [s08-probate-administration.md](./s08-probate-administration.md)).

## Cross-statute Relationships
- **Wills Act 1838** — testamentary-capacity threshold remains separate from MCA general-capacity threshold; the two coexist (see [s08-probate-administration.md](./s08-probate-administration.md)).
- **Women's Charter 1961** — incapacitated-spouse maintenance interfaces with MCA (see [s09-womens-charter-family.md](./s09-womens-charter-family.md)).
- **Legal Profession (Professional Conduct) Rules 2015** — duties to vulnerable clients and capacity-checking obligations attach when taking instructions.

## Practical Implications for a Solo Advocate
- **LPA + Will combo** is the standard estate-planning bundle. Encourage every Will client also to execute an LPA — the cost differential is small; the protective value is high.
- **CI sign-off** is a high-volume, low-margin service for solos. Build a quick capacity-screen and a contemporaneous file note for every Form 1 issued.
- **Decision-specific capacity:** a client may have capacity to make a Will but lack capacity to manage complex investments. Document the decision-specific assessment.
- **Deputy work** is procedurally heavy (accounts / reports / scope-limit applications) — quote fixed fees per stage to manage client expectations.
