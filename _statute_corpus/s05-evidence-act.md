# Statute Digest: Evidence Act 1893 — Singapore

- **Tier:** Tier 1 (Mandatory) — anchors admissibility for civil and criminal trials.

## Topic Digest

### A. Relevancy (Sections 5–57)
Only evidence of **relevant facts** is admissible. The Act provides express categories of relevance:
- **Res gestae** — facts forming part of the same transaction (s 6).
- **Motive, preparation, and previous or subsequent conduct** (s 8).
- **Similar fact** evidence (ss 14–15) — narrow gateway, especially in criminal cases.
- Facts showing **state of mind, body, or feelings** (s 14).

### B. Hearsay Rule and Key Exceptions
- **General rule:** oral evidence must be direct (s 62).
- **Statutory exceptions:** s 32 admits statements by persons who cannot be called (e.g., deceased declarants; persons whose attendance cannot be procured without unreasonable delay or expense); s 33 admits prior testimony where the witness is unavailable.

### C. Documentary Evidence (Sections 63–92)
- **Primary evidence** — the original document — is generally required (s 64).
- **Secondary evidence** — copies — is admissible only where the conditions in s 67 apply (original lost / in possession of opponent / public document, etc.).
- **Public documents** — s 74 categorises and ss 76–78 govern certified-copy proof.
- **Parol evidence rule** — ss 93–100 govern when extrinsic evidence may explain or vary a written contract.

### D. Burden of Proof (Sections 103–108)
- **Persuasive burden:** he who asserts must prove (s 103).
- **Evidential burden:** shifts during trial as facts are proved or unrebutted.
- **Civil standard:** balance of probabilities.
- **Criminal standard:** beyond reasonable doubt (a common-law overlay; the Act itself does not define the standard).

### E. Statutory Presumptions (Sections 111–116)
Includes:
- **s 114** — presumption of legitimacy of a child born during a valid marriage.
- **omnia praesumuntur rite esse acta** — presumption of regularity of official acts.
- **s 116** — power of the court to presume facts in the ordinary course of events.

### F. Witness Examination (Sections 137–168)
- **Examination-in-chief** — by the party calling the witness; leading questions generally prohibited (s 142).
- **Cross-examination** — by the opposing party; leading questions permitted (s 143).
- **Re-examination** — by the party calling the witness; confined to matters arising in cross.
- **Refreshing memory** — s 161 (contemporaneous writings).
- **Hostile witnesses** — s 156 (impeachment by the party calling).

### G. Privilege (Sections 124–133)
- **s 128** — Advocate-and-client communications privileged (subject to crime / fraud exception).
- **s 23 (read with s 24)** and the parallel common-law rule — "without prejudice" communications in settlement attempts are protected.
- **s 124** — official communications.
- **s 131** — communications in matrimonial matters.

## Verbatim Anchor Quote
> "Whoever desires any court to give judgment as to any legal right or liability, dependent on the existence of facts which he asserts, must prove that those facts exist."
> — Section 103, Evidence Act 1893.

## Practical Implications for a Solo Advocate
- **Primary-vs-secondary discipline:** serve every contract / WhatsApp screenshot / email as primary evidence where the original is available; build the s 67 foundation early if only secondary evidence will be tendered.
- **"Without prejudice" hygiene:** mark every settlement communication "WITHOUT PREJUDICE SAVE AS TO COSTS" if costs reservation is intended; otherwise plain "WITHOUT PREJUDICE."
- **AEIC drafting:** under ROC 2021, the AEIC is the entire evidence-in-chief for most civil trials — the Evidence Act admissibility filters apply to AEIC contents pre-trial (objections under O 15 r 19 / r 20 ROC).
