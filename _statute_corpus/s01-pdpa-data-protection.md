## Topic Digest

### A. Application & Scope
The PDPA governs the **collection, use, and disclosure of personal data by organisations**. It does NOT apply to:
- Individuals acting in a personal or domestic capacity.
- Public agencies (covered by the Public Sector (Governance) Act 2018 instead).
- Business contact information used for evaluating, establishing or maintaining a business relationship (carve-out).

"Personal data" means data, whether true or not, about an individual who can be identified from that data or from that data and other information to which the organisation has or is likely to have access.

### B. The Nine Data Protection Obligations
1. **Consent Obligation** — collection / use / disclosure generally requires consent (express, deemed, or under a specified exception — e.g., the Legitimate Interests exception and the Business Improvement exception, both introduced in 2020).
2. **Purpose Limitation Obligation** — only for purposes a reasonable person would consider appropriate in the circumstances and that have been notified to the individual.
3. **Notification Obligation** — purposes must be notified on or before collection.
4. **Access and Correction Obligation** — individuals may request access to and correction of their personal data.
5. **Accuracy Obligation** — reasonable effort to ensure accuracy where the data will be used to make a decision affecting the individual or disclosed to another organisation.
6. **Protection Obligation** — reasonable security arrangements to prevent unauthorised access, collection, use, disclosure, copying, modification or disposal.
7. **Retention Limitation Obligation** — cease retention when the purpose is no longer served and retention is no longer necessary.
8. **Transfer Limitation Obligation** — overseas transfer only where a comparable standard of protection applies.
9. **Accountability Obligation** — appoint a Data Protection Officer (DPO), develop policies and practices, make information about them available.

### C. Notifiable Data Breach (NDB) Regime (Part 6A, in force from 1 Feb 2021)
A data breach is **notifiable** if it (a) results in or is likely to result in significant harm to affected individuals; OR (b) is of a significant scale (default threshold: 500 or more affected individuals).

Notification timeline (after assessment that the breach is notifiable):
- **PDPC:** as soon as practicable, but **no later than 3 calendar days**.
- **Affected individuals:** as soon as practicable, in any manner reasonable in the circumstances (subject to limited exceptions — e.g., where remedial action removes the likelihood of significant harm).

Assessment itself must be conducted in a "reasonable and expeditious manner."

### D. Do Not Call (DNC) Provisions (Part 9)
Restricts the sending of specified marketing messages (voice calls / SMS / fax) to Singapore telephone numbers registered on the DNC Registry. Separate consent rules apply for marketing messages sent under the Spam Control Act 2007.

### E. Penalties
Maximum financial penalty for breach of the data-protection provisions:
- For an organisation with annual turnover in Singapore exceeding S$10 million: **up to 10% of annual turnover in Singapore**.
- Otherwise: **up to S$1 million**.
(Both figures introduced by the 2020 Amendment Act.)

## Verbatim Anchor Quote
> "An organisation shall not, on or after the appointed day, collect, use or disclose personal data about an individual unless — (a) the individual gives, or is deemed to have given, his consent under this Act to the collection, use or disclosure …"
> — Section 13, PDPA 2012.

## Cross-statute Relationships
- **Spam Control Act 2007** — parallel regime for unsolicited commercial electronic messages (works alongside DNC).
- **Cybersecurity Act 2018** — overlapping security obligations for designated Critical Information Infrastructure (CII).
- **Banking Act 1970 / Insurance Act 1966** — sector-specific confidentiality regimes co-exist with PDPA.
- **Public Sector (Governance) Act 2018** — replaces PDPA for public-sector data handling.

## Practical Implications for a Solo Advocate
- A solo law practice is an "organisation" under PDPA — DPO appointment, written internal policy, and a published privacy notice are mandatory.
- Common high-risk surfaces: cloud-hosted matter files (verify the cloud vendor's overseas-transfer posture), email forwarding to personal Gmail (Transfer + Protection risk), and shared drives accessible to non-advocate staff (Protection + Accountability risk).
- For client work: a single-incident "serious" data breach can trigger civil exposure under PDPA + parallel professional-conduct exposure under LPA Part 6 (see [s07-legal-profession-act.md](./s07-legal-profession-act.md)).
- The NDB 3-day timeline is hard. Build an internal incident-response checklist before an incident lands, not during one.
