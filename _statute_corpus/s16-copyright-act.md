# Statute Digest: Copyright Act 2021 — Singapore

PROVENANCE: CITED:_research/43-statute-copyright-intellectual-property.md

## Citation Block
- **Statute Name:** Copyright Act 2021
- **Official URL:** https://sso.agc.gov.sg/Act/CA2021
- **Regulator:** Intellectual Property Office of Singapore (IPOS) — https://www.ipos.gov.sg

## Authority Tier
- **Tier:** Tier 1 (Mandatory) for any practice touching content / publishing / technology / commissioned creative work.

## Currency Block
- **Commencement:** **21 November 2021** — the 2021 Act **replaced** the Copyright Act 1987.
- **Last Verified:** 2026-05-18.
- **Currency warning:** Default ownership of commissioned works **shifted to the creator** under the 2021 Act. Contracts signed under the 1987 Act may still follow the older "commissioner-owns" position. Always check the contract date and applicable transitional provisions.

## Language
- **Language:** English (Authoritative).

## Scope of Digest
- **Topics covered:** Default ownership shift (commissioned works); statutory right of attribution; "fair use" framework; computational data analysis exception; illicit-streaming offences; duration of copyright.

## Topic Digest

### A. Default Ownership Shift (Commissioned Works)
Under the **2021 Act**, the **creator** of a commissioned work (e.g., photographs, portraits, videos) is the **default owner** of the copyright, unless a written contract states otherwise. This **reverses** the 1987 Act position (where the commissioner was generally the default owner for prescribed commissioned categories).

Implication: every commissioning agreement must now expressly assign copyright to the commissioner if commissioner-ownership is intended.

### B. Right of Attribution
A statutory right of attribution — for **authors of works** and **performers of performances** — to be **identified** when the work / performance is publicly used. The right:
- **Cannot be assigned** (it is personal to the author / performer).
- **Can be waived** in writing.

### C. Fair Use (Replacing "Fair Dealing")
The 2021 Act introduces a **"fair use"** framework, replacing the earlier "fair dealing" regime. The four-factor test continues:
1. The purpose and character of the use (including whether commercial / non-profit / educational).
2. The nature of the work.
3. The amount and substantiality of the portion used.
4. The effect on the potential market for, or value of, the work.

The pre-2021 factor regarding the **reasonable availability** of the work has been removed.

### D. Computational Data Analysis Exception
A specific exception permits use of copyrighted works for **computational data analysis** (text and data mining) — including, in suitable cases, AI / model-training use — provided the access to the work was **lawful**. The exception is conditioned (e.g., on lawful access; on the data analysis not being for commercial exploitation in certain ways) and should be checked against the operative section before reliance.

### E. Illicit Streaming
Civil and criminal liability attaches to those who **sell, distribute, or deal in** set-top boxes, devices, or services that facilitate access to infringing content (e.g., unauthorised over-the-top streaming).

### F. Duration of Copyright
- **Literary, dramatic, musical, artistic works:** generally **life of the author + 70 years**.
- Specific durations for sound recordings, broadcasts, films, and performances differ; verify per category.

## Practical Implications for a Solo Advocate
- **Commissioning Agreements / Master Service Agreements** for creative work **must explicitly address ownership** if the client wants commissioner-ownership. The default no longer does the work.
- **Law-firm websites** must take care: stock photos, commissioned headshots, and contributed articles attract attribution rights unless waived in writing.
- **AI / model-training advice:** the computational-data-analysis exception is contingent on lawful access; "scraped" content from paywalled or terms-of-service-protected sources does not get the benefit.
- **Pre-2021 contracts** that pre-date the new default — read against the old Act and any transitional provisions.
