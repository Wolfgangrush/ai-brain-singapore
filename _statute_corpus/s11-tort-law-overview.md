## Topic Digest

### A. Negligence — The Spandeck Test
Singapore's unified duty-of-care framework is the **two-stage Spandeck test**:
1. **Proximity** — sufficient closeness between the parties (physical, circumstantial, or causal).
2. **Policy** — no countervailing public-policy reasons to deny the duty.

This test applies to **all** novel negligence claims (pure economic loss, psychiatric harm, omissions) — a unifying departure from older English authority.

### B. Defamation
- **Common-law elements:** a statement that (1) is defamatory, (2) refers to the plaintiff, and (3) is published to a third party.
- **Statutory framework:** Defamation Act 1957 codifies defences and procedural rules.
- **Defences:**
  - **Justification / truth.**
  - **Fair comment** (now: honest comment).
  - **Privilege** — absolute (court proceedings, parliamentary speech) and qualified (e.g., references; statements made in performance of a duty).
- **Limitation:** **1 year** for defamation actions (special period — NOT the general 6-year tort period under the Limitation Act).

### C. Nuisance and Trespass
- **Private nuisance** — substantial and unreasonable interference with the use and enjoyment of land.
- **Public nuisance** — nuisance affecting a class of the public; plaintiff must show particular damage.
- **Trespass to land** — direct, intentional, and unauthorised entry.
- **Trespass to person** — assault, battery, false imprisonment.

### D. Vicarious Liability
Employers are generally liable for torts committed by employees **in the course of their employment**. The "close connection" test from the modern English authorities is followed locally with appropriate refinement.

### E. Contributory Negligence
Statutory regime under s 3 CNPIA 1953 — damages are reduced in proportion to the plaintiff's own fault. Apportionment is a question of fact.

### F. Psychiatric Harm
Recoverable where the plaintiff establishes:
- a **recognisable psychiatric illness** (mere grief or distress is not enough);
- meets proximity / foreseeability thresholds (primary vs secondary victim distinctions apply, with local refinement).

### G. Professional Negligence
- **Medical:** Bolam-Bolitho framework — competent body of medical opinion subject to logical scrutiny.
- **Lawyers and other professionals:** the Bolam standard generally applies for the standard of care. The duty of care arises from the retainer (and may extend to non-clients in limited circumstances — Spandeck-tested).

## Verbatim Anchor Quote
> "Where any person suffers damage as the result partly of his own fault and partly of the fault of any other person … the damages recoverable in respect thereof shall be reduced to such extent as the court thinks just and equitable …"
> — Section 3(1), Contributory Negligence and Personal Injuries Act 1953.

## Cross-statute Relationships
- **Limitation Act 1959** — 3-year period for personal injury; 1-year period for defamation; 6-year period for other torts (see [s06-limitation-act.md](./s06-limitation-act.md)).
- **Evidence Act 1893** — expert-evidence and causation-evidence rules (see [s05-evidence-act.md](./s05-evidence-act.md)).
- **PDPA 2012** — overlap with privacy-tort-style claims; PDPA does not create a private right of action for tortious damages, but s 48O introduces a private right of action for emotional-harm-causing breaches (verify scope at PDPC level) (see [s01-pdpa-data-protection.md](./s01-pdpa-data-protection.md)).
- **POHA 2014** — parallel statutory framework for harassment, stalking, and false statements; provides Protection Orders and statutory damages.

## Practical Implications for a Solo Advocate
- **Personal injury intake:** apply the 3-year PI limitation period from the date of injury or date of knowledge — diary both candidate dates.
- **Defamation intake:** apply the **1-year** limitation period — file fast or lose.
- **Professional-negligence files** (e.g., medical or legal) require early independent expert instruction; the Bolam-Bolitho framework is evidentially expert-driven.
- **Online-defamation** matters frequently combine claims under defamation + POHA + (sometimes) breach of confidence — pleading multiple causes broadens remedy options.
