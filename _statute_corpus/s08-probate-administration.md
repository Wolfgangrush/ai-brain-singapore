## Topic Digest

### A. Grant of Probate
Granted where there is a **valid will**. The Executor named in the will applies to the court for a grant of probate to administer the estate in accordance with the will.

### B. Letters of Administration (LOA)
Granted where the deceased died **intestate** (no will). The court appoints an Administrator — usually the surviving spouse or, failing that, an entitled next-of-kin. Distribution follows the Intestate Succession Act.

### C. Letters of Administration with Will Annexed
Granted where there is a will but no executor is named, willing, or able to act. The court appoints an Administrator who must distribute according to the will.

### D. Intestate Succession Act 1967 — Distribution Rules
- **Spouse only, no issue or parents:** spouse takes the whole estate.
- **Spouse + issue:** spouse 50%, issue 50% (per stirpes).
- **Spouse + parents, no issue:** spouse 50%, parents 50%.
- **Issue only:** issue take the whole (per stirpes).
- (Further classes — siblings, more distant kin — under s 7 ISA.)

### E. Wills Act 1838 — Validity Formalities
A valid will must be:
- in **writing**;
- **signed** by the testator (or by another in the testator's presence and by their direction); AND
- the signature **acknowledged** in the presence of **two or more witnesses**, present at the same time, who attest and sign.

A witness (or their spouse) cannot benefit under the will (the gift fails, though the will itself survives).

### F. Jurisdictional Allocation
- **Family Justice Courts (FJC):** estates with value up to S$5 million.
- **General Division of the High Court (Family Division):** estates above S$5 million.
- Verify the current threshold at FJC practice direction level before filing — thresholds shift over time.

### G. Public Trustee — Small Estates
Where the estate value is below **S$50,000** and no grant has been taken out, the Public Trustee may administer the estate without a formal grant (see Public Trustee Act).

## Cross-statute Relationships
- **Mental Capacity Act 2008** — Lasting Power of Attorney (LPA) is the lifetime counterpart to a Will (see [s14-mental-capacity-act.md](./s14-mental-capacity-act.md)).
- **Land Titles Act 1993** — joint-tenancy property passes by survivorship (does NOT pass under the Will); tenancy-in-common share does pass under the Will (see [s12-property-land-law.md](./s12-property-land-law.md)).
- **CPF Act** — CPF moneys pass by CPF nomination (separate regime — not under the Will).
- **Insurance (Nominations) framework** — nominated life-insurance moneys pass separately.

## Practical Implications for a Solo Advocate
- Probate / Wills is a high-volume "bread and butter" area for solo practices. Workflow is highly procedural — adherence to FJC eLitigation forms (Caveat search, Schedule of Assets, Oath of Executor) drives most of the work.
- **Property holding mode** is a critical pre-Will conversation: joint-tenancy vs tenancy-in-common materially changes what the Will controls.
- **CPF nomination** must be discussed separately from the Will — clients regularly assume the Will covers CPF, and it does not.
- **Witness independence** — witnesses to the Will should not be beneficiaries (or spouses of beneficiaries) — the gift to the beneficiary fails.
