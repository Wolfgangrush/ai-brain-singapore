## Topic Digest

### A. Torrens System
Singapore land is nearly 100% under the Torrens system. The **Land Register** maintained by the SLA is the definitive proof of ownership; "title by registration" rather than "registration of title." Once an interest is registered, the register reflects the legal position absent statutory exception.

### B. Indefeasibility — Section 46 LTA
A registered proprietor holds the estate or interest **free from all encumbrances, liens, estates, or interests** other than those expressly noted on the register and the statutory exceptions (e.g., fraud to which the proprietor is party).

### C. Estates and Interests
- **Estate in Fee Simple** — perpetual ownership.
- **Leasehold** — common in Singapore (e.g., 99-year and 999-year leases on residential developments; 60-year leases for some commercial / industrial).
- **Statutory Land Grant** — perpetual but subject to grant conditions (Crown-grant analogue, historical context).

### D. Caveats (Section 115 LTA)
A **caveat** is a register-recorded "freeze" lodged by a person claiming an interest in the land (e.g., a buyer who has paid the option fee, a beneficiary of a contract for sale). The caveat does not create the interest; it warns third parties and forces engagement before further dealings.

### E. Joint Tenancy vs. Tenancy-in-Common
- **Joint Tenancy** — the four unities (possession, interest, title, time); right of survivorship — on the death of one joint tenant, the share passes to the surviving joint tenant(s) and does **NOT** form part of the deceased's estate under the Will.
- **Tenancy-in-Common** — each owner holds a distinct, devisable share; on death, the share passes under the Will (or under Intestate Succession Act if no Will — see [s08-probate-administration.md](./s08-probate-administration.md)).

### F. Mortgages and Charges
Must be registered as a **Memorandum of Mortgage** to be enforceable against the land. Default + sale-by-mortgagee processes are statute- and contract-driven; mortgagee duty of care to mortgagor on sale applies.

### G. Strata Title
Governed by the **Land Titles (Strata) Act 1967** for condominiums and apartments with shared facilities. The Management Corporation Strata Title (MCST) administers common property; the Strata Titles Boards adjudicate disputes.

## Verbatim Anchor Quote
> "Any person who is registered as proprietor of an estate or interest in land … shall hold that estate or interest free from all encumbrances, liens, estates, or interests whatsoever …"
> — Section 46, Land Titles Act 1993.

## Cross-statute Relationships
- **Probate and Administration regime** — joint-tenancy vs tenancy-in-common holding mode controls what passes under the Will (see [s08-probate-administration.md](./s08-probate-administration.md)).
- **Stamp Duties Act 1929** — stamping is a separate prerequisite to many conveyancing instruments.
- **CPF Act** — CPF-funded purchases interact with the LTA at the charge layer.
- **Electronic Transactions Act 2010** — Schedule 1 excludes "indentures and deeds for the sale or transfer of immovable property" from electronic-signature recognition; conveyancing instruments remain wet-ink (see [s17-eta-electronic-transactions.md](./s17-eta-electronic-transactions.md)).

## Practical Implications for a Solo Advocate
- **SLA Title Search** is the first practical step on every conveyancing intake — confirms the registered proprietor, encumbrances, caveats, and restrictive covenants.
- **Caveat timing** is critical — failure to lodge promptly after the option-fee payment exposes the buyer to subsequent dealings.
- **Holding-mode advice** at the buying stage drives Will-stage outcomes years later; this is a frequent estate-planning omission.
- **e-Lodgment discipline** — most conveyancing now runs through SLA's electronic systems; clerical errors (incorrect parties, mistyped consideration) are difficult and expensive to remediate post-lodgment.
