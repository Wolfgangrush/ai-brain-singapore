## Topic Digest

### A. Omnibus Structure
IRDA consolidates **personal insolvency** (bankruptcy) and **corporate insolvency** (winding-up, judicial management, schemes of arrangement, simplified procedures) into a single statute, with shared provisions on insolvency practitioners and the Insolvency Office.

### B. Personal Bankruptcy
- **Statutory demand / debt threshold:** a debtor can be made bankrupt where the debt is at least **S$15,000** (s 311 IRDA).
- **Process:** statutory demand → debtor's failure to satisfy within 21 days → bankruptcy application by the creditor.
- **Discharge:** automatic discharge regimes apply on prescribed conditions and timelines.

### C. Corporate Winding-Up
Two modes:
- **Voluntary winding-up** — by members (solvent) or creditors (insolvent).
- **Compulsory winding-up by court** — most commonly on the ground of inability to pay debts (s 125(2) IRDA — statutory demand unpaid for 3 weeks creates a deemed inability).

### D. Judicial Management
A **rescue procedure** to preserve the company as a going concern, providing a **statutory moratorium** against legal proceedings while the Judicial Manager attempts a restructuring or sale of the business. Replaces (and refines) the prior CA 1967 judicial-management provisions.

### E. Schemes of Arrangement
Court-supervised process for compromises and arrangements between a company and its creditors. IRDA introduced enhancements modelled in part on US Chapter 11 (e.g., automatic moratorium, super-priority rescue financing).

### F. Ipso Facto Clause Restrictions — Section 440
Restricts a counterparty from terminating or modifying a contract solely because the other party has commenced restructuring proceedings (e.g., entered judicial management or proposed a scheme). Subject to prescribed exceptions.

### G. Wrongful Trading — Section 239
Directors can be **personally liable** for debts incurred by an insolvent company where the director knew or ought reasonably to have known that there was no reasonable prospect of avoiding insolvent liquidation. Important read-with: directors' duties under s 157 CA 1967 — see [s03-companies-act.md](./s03-companies-act.md).

### H. Simplified Restructuring + Simplified Winding-Up (Part 10A)
Lower-cost, prescribed-eligibility programmes for **Micro and Small Companies (MSCs)** — designed to reduce cost / complexity for small businesses entering restructuring or winding-up.

## Verbatim Anchor Quote
> "A company is deemed to be unable to pay its debts if a creditor … has served on the company … a demand … requiring the company to pay the sum so due and the company has for 3 weeks thereafter neglected to pay the sum …"
> — Section 125(2), IRDA 2018.

## Cross-statute Relationships
- **Companies Act 1967** — director duties + corporate-governance backbone; migration of insolvency provisions to IRDA does not remove CA 1967 directors' duties (see [s03-companies-act.md](./s03-companies-act.md)).
- **Bankruptcy Act (repealed)** — pre-2020 bankruptcy proceedings remain governed by transitional provisions.
- **CLPA / Land Titles Act** — secured-creditor enforcement against immovable property interacts with IRDA priority rules (see [s12-property-land-law.md](./s12-property-land-law.md)).

## Practical Implications for a Solo Advocate
- **Statutory demand** is the bread-and-butter pre-litigation tool for debt-recovery — fast, cheap, high-pressure; verify the S$15,000 threshold (personal) and undisputed-debt requirement before issuing.
- **Wrongful-trading risk to directors** is a critical client-counselling moment when an SME slides toward insolvency — advise on the timing of director-level decisions to seek professional advice / scheme protection.
- **MSC simplified routes** are under-used — many small businesses default to full winding-up when the simplified route would have closed the file more cheaply.
- **Ipso facto** restrictions affect counter-party drafting strategy — contract clauses purporting to terminate on the other side's restructuring filing may be unenforceable.
