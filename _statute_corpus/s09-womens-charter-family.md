## Topic Digest

### A. Sole Ground for Divorce — Irretrievable Breakdown
The Women's Charter recognises **one ground** for divorce: irretrievable breakdown of marriage. It must be proved by one of **six facts**:
1. **Adultery** — and the petitioner finds it intolerable to live with the respondent.
2. **Unreasonable behaviour** — respondent has behaved in such a way that the petitioner cannot reasonably be expected to live with the respondent.
3. **Desertion** — for at least 2 continuous years.
4. **Separation with consent** — at least 3 years' continuous separation, with the respondent's consent.
5. **Separation without consent** — at least 4 years' continuous separation.
6. **Divorce by Mutual Agreement (DMA)** — new sixth fact (July 2024); requires a joint statement of reasons for the breakdown and evidence of reconciliation efforts attempted.

### B. Divorce by Mutual Agreement (DMA) — Section 95(3)
Allows parties to **jointly** state that their marriage has irretrievably broken down, providing reasons and showing the efforts (if any) at reconciliation. The DMA pathway is non-fault and is positioned as the "therapeutic justice" route.

### C. Ancillary Matters
On a divorce, the court may make orders on:
- **Custody, care and control, and access** in respect of children.
- **Maintenance** for the wife / former wife / incapacitated husband (s 113) and the children (s 127).
- **Division of matrimonial assets** (s 112).
- Other connected reliefs (e.g., dealing with the matrimonial home).

### D. Maintenance — Section 113
The court may order a man to pay maintenance to his wife or former wife; reciprocally, women may be ordered to pay maintenance to an **incapacitated husband** (the gender symmetry is partial — verify the latest provision before pleading).

### E. Division of Matrimonial Assets — Section 112
Courts apply a **structured approach** to identify, value, and divide matrimonial assets — weighing **direct financial contributions** against **indirect contributions** (including homemaker / caregiver contributions). The split is not formulaic; the court arrives at a "just and equitable" division on the facts.

### F. Mandatory Parenting Programme (MPP)
For parties with **minor children** who cannot agree on ancillary matters, completion of the MPP is required **before** filing for divorce.

### G. Personal Protection Orders (PPO)
Available to victims of **family violence** as defined under Pt VII of the Charter. The court may also grant Domestic Exclusion Orders and Counselling Orders.

## Verbatim Anchor Quote
> "The court may … make a judgment of divorce if it is satisfied that the parties to the marriage have reached a mutual agreement that their marriage has irretrievably broken down."
> — Section 95(3), Women's Charter 1961.

## Practical Implications for a Solo Advocate
- The DMA route **changes the negotiation posture** — much family work is now upstream consensus-building rather than fact-adversarial pleading. Solos should pivot toward Therapeutic Justice (TJ) competence.
- **MPP** is a hard pre-filing gate where minor children are involved — diary the MPP completion certificate before drafting the filing.
- **s 112 indirect contributions** are evidence-heavy — a homemaker spouse needs contemporaneous evidence of contributions (school-run records, household expense receipts, caregiving testimony) collected on intake.
