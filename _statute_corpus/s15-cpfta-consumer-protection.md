## Topic Digest

### A. Unfair Practices — Section 4
A supplier engages in an **unfair practice** where, in relation to a consumer transaction, the supplier:
- does or says anything (or omits anything) such that, as a result, a consumer might reasonably be deceived or misled;
- makes a false claim;
- takes advantage of a consumer in a way that would be unfair if the consumer is not reasonably able to protect their interests by reason of disability, illiteracy, etc.

The Second Schedule contains a non-exhaustive list of **specific unfair practices** (false representations about quality, sponsorship, place of origin; bait-and-switch tactics; pressure-sale tactics; etc.).

### B. Consumer Right to Sue — Section 6
A consumer may bring a civil action against a supplier for unfair-practice conduct.

### C. Monetary Threshold
Claims are generally limited to a **maximum value of S$30,000** in the State Courts' consumer-claims jurisdiction. Higher-value claims must be brought in the ordinary civil-claims track.

### D. CASE / STB Mediation Pathway
- **CASE** mediates consumer disputes voluntarily; CASE can also pursue Voluntary Compliance Agreements with errant retailers.
- **STB (Singapore Tourism Board)** plays an analogous mediation role for transactions with tourism-related suppliers.

### E. CCCS Enforcement
The CCCS may take **court action** against persistently errant retailers to obtain orders restraining unfair practices — public-interest enforcement separate from any individual consumer's right to sue.

### F. Cooling-Off Period
Mandatory cooling-off applies to specified contracts — **direct sales**, **time-share** contracts, and prescribed motor-vehicle-related contracts. Within the cooling-off window (typically 5 working days, but verify per category), a consumer may withdraw without penalty.

### G. Lemon Law (Part III)
Where goods sold to a consumer do not conform to contract within **6 months of delivery**, there is a statutory presumption that they were non-conforming at delivery. Remedies follow a tiered hierarchy:
- **Repair or replacement** (consumer's choice, subject to disproportionality).
- **Reduction in price**.
- **Rescission** of the contract (refund).

## Verbatim Anchor Quote
> "It is an unfair practice for a supplier, in relation to a consumer transaction — (a) to do or say anything … if as a result a consumer might reasonably be deceived or misled …"
> — Section 4, Consumer Protection (Fair Trading) Act 2003.

## Cross-statute Relationships
- **Sale of Goods Act 1979** — implied terms in sale-of-goods contracts (overlapping consumer protection layer; UCTA also bears).
- **Unfair Contract Terms Act 1977 (UCTA)** — exclusion-clause control (see [s02-contract-law-overview.md](./s02-contract-law-overview.md)).
- **Misrepresentation Act 1967** — alternative cause of action where the unfair-practice claim is borderline.
- **Limitation Act 1959** — 6-year contract / tort period (see [s06-limitation-act.md](./s06-limitation-act.md)).

## Practical Implications for a Solo Advocate
- For **small retailers**, audit marketing copy, online listings, and direct-sales scripts for the Second-Schedule unfair-practice triggers; rectify pre-emptively.
- For **consumer clients**, CPFTA is a faster, cheaper litigation track than general contract law for small-value disputes (especially below the S$30,000 cap).
- **Lemon Law** is widely under-used because consumers default to "warranty" framing — the statutory presumption within 6 months materially shifts the burden of proof.
- **Cooling-off** is consumer-friendly but seller-error-prone — verify product / contract category against the cooling-off-applicable list before relying on it.
